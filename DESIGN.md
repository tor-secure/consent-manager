## Components

### Buttons and fields

Primary buttons have solid teal fill; secondary and outline variants use card surfaces and input-colored borders. Ghost buttons use muted text and reveal a muted background on hover. ### Navigation and panels

The navy sidebar uses light text, rounded links, and a muted teal active surface. The header is a solid card surface. Panels group related work with borders; avoid adding unrelated card elevation.

### Provenance and evidence

Badges display observed, configured, inferred, enforced, or unknown text with a small dot. The evidence timeline has a thin vertical rule, circular markers, tabular timestamps, and readable event details. Detail drawers preserve context and offer a named close control.

### Privacy Graph

The SVG map supports keyboard node selection, visible selection, search, entity/provenance filters, and a relationship inspector. Its rendered map is capped at 40 entities and 180 connecting relationships. The inspector displays up to 100 relationships, and the list offers progressive disclosure. Keep these limits visible. Purpose impact previews are read-only requests; their loading, result, and error states must remain explicit.

### Digital Twin

The dependency map is the default. Choosing 3D exploration loads the topology component; that component imports Three.js and OrbitControls on mount. It shows up to 80 configured entities and considers up to 300 edges. Rotation, zoom and reset buttons accompany pointer controls and an ordinary entity selector.

Rendering responds to interaction and visibility; there is no automatic rotation. WebGL startup or context errors explain that the dependency map remains available. Keep all entity inspection possible through ordinary controls.

### Motion and public preview

MotionProvider uses a 0.2-second transition and user reduced-motion preference. Drawer entry translates 24px with opacity; reduced motion removes translation. PageTransition adds a 0.35-second route entrance from 80% opacity and 8px down. PageExperience observes newly visible public sections and workspace blocks, entering from 80% opacity and 16px down over 0.5 seconds; it skips hidden and exceptionally tall blocks, runs once per observed element and completes active animations on cleanup or a reduced-motion preference change. Both start from visible server-rendered content. CSS reduced-motion rules minimize transition and animation durations. Floating previews and marquees remain disabled; the experience layer adds short entrances and selected link hover movement.

Public hero preview states explain the product with an illustrative diagram. They must not be presented as live tenant results.

**The Unique Model Rule.** Give every DPDP chapter its own detailed object and mechanism; reserve the orb and rings for the Overview hero.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** reuse scoped theme variables and existing logo assets.
- **Do** preserve written provenance, visible focus, and readable alternatives to 3D.
- **Do** use Motion for short user-triggered state changes and honor reduced motion.
- **Do** distinguish fixture screenshots and illustrative public diagrams from live evidence.
- **Do** preserve public factual copy, pricing terms, links, and legal content.

### Don't:

- **Don't** add decorative continuous motion or motion that steers consent choices.
- **Don't** invent metrics, evidence, enforcement status, or backend capabilities.
- **Don't** hide graph truncation limits or make 3D the only inspection path.
- **Don't** assume public fixed-color sections automatically support dashboard dark tokens.
